# Data Analysis Process

Turn a business question into a supported decision using the supplied data and context. Scale the work to the request: a narrow metric check may need only a few checks, while a multi-source investigation needs a traceable preparation and analysis record. Resume from an existing sound stage rather than restarting every stage.

## Scope and access

- Work in the selected project's lane and use only its authorized sources. Keep raw data, working files and outputs there; a shared skill is not a shared client dataset. Ask for the lane if it is materially ambiguous.
- Preserve raw inputs. Keep transformations reproducible and minimize personal data. Do not copy private examples into the shared library.
- This skill does not authorize connecting new sources, sending a corpus to an external tool, indexing, publishing, contacting people, spending or changing campaigns. Use existing task authorization and access boundaries.
- Covered here: objective, collection, cleaning, processing, insights and priorities. The full polar/secondary-metric framework, macro/micro funnels, analysis flows/reporting and separate lead-generation, e-commerce, affiliate and digital-product tracks are not supplied. Do not fabricate their course rules or claim full-course coverage.

## Work from the decision

Use the stages as an adaptable workflow. Read [the method](references/method.md) when selecting sources, transforming data or interpreting evidence; use its relevant sections rather than applying every check to every question.

1. **Set the objective.** Identify the problem, decision, measurable question, scope, period and segment. Record definitions, baseline and desired target where known. Reuse supplied context; ask only for missing information that changes the analysis. Seek evidence against as well as for the initial explanation.
2. **Collect relevant data.** Select sources by the question. Record provenance, extraction date, grain, reporting period, currency, timezone, event/status definitions and attribution. Check coverage and comparability before merging; retain separate views when reconciliation is not defensible.
3. **Clean without concealing uncertainty.** Investigate missing values, duplicates, outliers, formatting and inconsistent labels. Log the rules, affected records and changes in counts/totals. Unknown values remain unknown; valid unusual records remain available.
4. **Process in context.** Calculate only relevant measures using compatible numerators and denominators. Preserve cohort and source definitions, recompute aggregate ratios, and annotate meaningful offer, price, stock, traffic, campaign and measurement changes. Use pivots, charts and comparable benchmarks when they help answer the question.
5. **Get insights.** Separate observations, possible explanations and supported causal findings. State sample, period, uncertainty and limitations. A small sample, funnel drop, correlation, repeated-why chain or visual pattern cannot establish a winner or root cause on its own. Use an inconclusive result when warranted and propose evidence or a bounded test needed to resolve it.
6. **Prioritize the next decision.** Explain business impact, urgency, feasibility, dependencies, resources and confidence. Record an owner and next step when known; rankings are suggestions. A high-impact urgent issue with low feasibility needs a resource/dependency decision, not an unqualified quick-win label.

If usable data are missing, deliver the supported partial result and a precise collection or reconciliation plan. Do not invent values to finish the workflow.

## Deliver what the decision needs

Use a concise answer, working table or analysis artifact appropriate to the request. Include enough evidence to review the conclusion: the question and scope; source/definition and cleaning notes; relevant calculations and comparisons; observations separated from hypotheses; uncertainty; and an explained next action or priority. Link local evidence when available. This is a minimum evidence contract, not a prescribed reporting format.

Before delivery, reconcile important source and transformed totals, check units and denominators, and verify that the proposed action follows from the evidence. State unresolved gaps and what would change the conclusion. Do not present a target, estimate or forecast as an observed result or a guaranteed outcome.
