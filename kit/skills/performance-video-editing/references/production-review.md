# Video editing review notes

Owner-requested standing review. Read before planning or editing a video and again before judging its exported result. Apply the relevant checks to the requested scope; do not turn them into new approval requirements. Keep client scripts, brand assets, evidence and taste decisions in the selected client lane.

## Before production

- State the viewer, one communication goal, core problem or desire, supported benefit, intended placement and next action. Preserve the supplied script and its approval state. Flag a weak hook separately; do not silently replace approved copy.
- Map each meaningful spoken beat to something the viewer can actually see or understand. A background photograph may establish context, but it does not demonstrate a product mechanism or prove an outcome.
- When using a visual reference, break down representative shots: opening image, information revealed, text hierarchy, motion purpose, cuts, sound and ending. Record whether access covered stills, transcripts or full audiovisual playback. Adapt the principles to the current brand.
- For a substantial new edit, settle key visual frames and try a low-cost animatic using the supplied recording or an explicitly provisional timing reference before polishing the entire video. Use it to test comprehension, framing, shot lengths and pacing. This does not require a new synthetic recording or provider call. A tiny correction does not need a new storyboard.

## Recurring mistakes to check

| Mistake | Decision/check |
| --- | --- |
| Benefit stated but never shown | Include relevant authentic evidence or a clearly labelled explanatory diagram of the relationship being explained. Do not present an invented product screen as an actual screenshot or verified feature. Never fabricate results or testimonials. |
| Still images and type movement substitute for visual progression | Check what changes in the viewer's understanding at each beat. Stills, hard cuts and restrained motion remain valid when they serve the idea; effects are not a quota. |
| Generic opening only names the audience | Check whether the first image and line give the intended viewer a specific reason to continue. Assess relevance and curiosity without claiming measured retention. |
| Execution starts without a visual plan | Use key frames/animatic for new substantial work; resolve major sequencing problems before detailed animation. |
| Reference reduced to colors and fonts | Study the reference's explanation, timing and sound as well as its appearance. Do not claim to have watched inaccessible material. |
| Largest text highlights a generic word | Give visual priority to the current beat's important meaning. Test hierarchy, contrast and actual phone-size readability, including Arabic joining and mixed direction. |
| Ending consumes time without purpose | Balance the CTA's reading time against the full edit. Review pauses after speech and any long static hold. No universal cut frequency or ending percentage. |
| CTA action and destination do not connect | Match spoken/on-screen action to the real next step, platform button and destination. Mention entry conditions only when confirmed and useful. |
| Voice processing replaces sound direction and listening | Design delivery and picture together. Time the edit against the actual recording. Check product-name pronunciation, pace, sentence tails, noise and mix. Distinguish human listening from model audio assessment and signal measurements. Music/SFX are optional. |
| A vertical frame is assumed safe for every placement | Check important copy, logos, faces and proof against the intended platform's actual overlays/preview. Record untested placements; do not claim obstruction without observing it. |
| Technical tests automatically declare creative approval | Automated checks emit measurements, never a hardcoded visual or listening pass. Inspect the actual export, not only code or contact sheets. Keep technical, perceptual, creative and owner-approval states distinct. |
| Manual timing, processing and delivery copies drift | For reusable production keep an authoritative edit plan and reproducible audio preparation. Derive or validate dependent timings against real media. Verify the exact named delivery file and keep versioned reviews tied to its hash. |

## Asset and timing decisions

Choose real footage/UI, generated raster imagery, practical material, graphics or code according to what the scene must communicate. Do not default to drawing every asset in code, or to generating every asset. Inspect generated assets before use and preserve their provenance. Keep accurate editable typography, numbers and original logos separate where control is needed.

Use measured speech timing, not estimated duration alone. Phrase-level captions and visual labels have different roles; do not force word-by-word captions or a particular transition library. Inventory tools by tested capability, not installed/transitive package names.

## Before delivery

1. Check technical properties and full decode of the exact delivered export. Compare actual per-channel audio peaks; a downmix with added gain is not evidence of source clipping.
2. Inspect opening, transitions, typography, key demonstration/proof, CTA and ending at phone size. Add normal-speed motion/playback and listening checks when available. If a model reviews audio/video, record that method precisely; still frames and waveform similarity do not establish natural delivery or perceived motion quality.
3. Critique communication and execution separately from render validity. Correct observed defects within the authorized scope, rerender affected material and review the new export. Do not apply an unsuitable automated suggestion merely to claim a revision.
4. Record each review with artifact path/hash, version, reviewer/method, inspected ranges, findings, corrections and unresolved checks. Technical reruns must preserve earlier review records and must not transfer a prior creative verdict to changed bytes. Only mark actual checks completed.
5. Deliver a usable link/preview of the same checked file. Describe its real state: concept, animatic, production test or reviewed export. Do not infer owner taste approval, platform readiness or advertising performance from a technical pass.

These notes guide decisions; they do not authorize purchases, provider calls, installs, uploads or publishing beyond the user's current instruction. Record material results in the selected client's required checkpoint, respecting the task's write scope and applicable workspace instructions; the note itself does not authorize unrelated writes during a read-only task. Promote a client lesson into this generic note only when the owner requests reuse or when the transferable method is separately authorized.
