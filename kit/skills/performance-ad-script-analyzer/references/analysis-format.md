## 3. Creative Framework Identification

Identify the main framework, with a secondary one only when useful. Candidates include:

- Problem → Agitate → Solution; Hook → Problem → Solution → CTA.
- Before / After; Storytelling; UGC Testimonial; Product Demonstration.

Explain which framework is actually used, why it fits or does not fit the audience, how transitions work, and whether an alternative is worth testing. An alternative could outperform; do not say it will without results.

## 4. Hook Analysis — FIRST 3 SECONDS

Separate the opening from the rest. Estimate what could be delivered in the first three seconds at the likely pace; do not assume the entire opening paragraph fits.

Evaluate:

- Immediate curiosity, relevance, emotion or tension.
- Speed with which the viewer recognizes “this is for me”.
- Specificity versus generic wording, and whether it sounds like an ad.
- Whether it reveals too much too early.
- Presence of an open loop and reason to hear the next sentence.
- Verbal and visual execution potential, distinguishing proposed footage from supplied footage.

Give **one hook score from 0–10**, with a short reason. Supply **3 stronger hook variations using different psychological angles**, each labeled by angle. Strength is a reasoned proposal, not a verified performance result.

## 5. Retention Analysis

Analyze likely watch-time and retention risks at line or section level:

- Likely drop-off points and why viewers might leave there.
- Long sentences, repetition, predictable sections and weak transitions.
- Information overload and lines that close curiosity too early.
- Material to move earlier or later.
- Suitable pattern interrupts and honest open loops.

For each major risk, quote or locate it, explain **why** it could hurt retention and give a concrete edit or visual response. Do not invent an observed retention curve.

## 6. Persuasion & Conversion Analysis

Evaluate the following separately, proportionate to the goal:

- **Problem:** Is the pain/desire specific and strong enough?
- **Product:** Is what is being sold or offered obvious?
- **Value Proposition:** Does the viewer quickly understand why to care?
- **Mechanism:** Is it clear why/how the product creates the desired result?
- **Benefits:** Does the script explain outcomes or only features?
- **Proof:** Is there actual credibility, demonstration, social proof, specificity or evidence?
- **Objections:** Which material objections remain unanswered?
- **Offer:** Is there a real, relevant reason to act?
- **CTA:** Is the next step clear and convincing?

For trust/follower scripts, discuss the value of watching/following and distinguish it from an immediate purchase. Do not demand that one short video address every possible objection.

## 7. Audience & Awareness Analysis

Choose and justify a likely awareness level:

**Unaware / Problem Aware / Solution Aware / Product Aware / Most Aware.**

Treat this as a messaging inference, not a demographic truth or an audience setting. Explain:

- Whether the messaging matches this awareness level.
- Who the script will likely resonate with most.
- Who it will likely not resonate with.
- The customer belief the script tries to change.
- The belief needed before the intended conversion or next step.

## 8. What Works Well

Identify exact elements worth keeping and explain why each could contribute to performance. Consider strong lines, hook, curiosity, emotion, specificity, positioning, persuasion, proof, objections, offer, CTA and natural/native language. Do not give generic compliments or call a creative a winner without evidence.

## 9. Weaknesses / What Should Be Improved

Use the sequence **Problem → Why it hurts performance → How to fix it** for every weakness. Inspect:

- Generic hooks, slow introductions and ad-like language.
- Unnecessary sentences, weak transitions and missing curiosity.
- Lack of specificity or emotional connection.
- Features without benefits; missing mechanism, proof or objection response.
- Weak offer or CTA relative to the actual objective.
- Drop-off risks, unbelievable claims and sections to remove.

Be direct and selective. Do not manufacture defects to fill a checklist. Section 9 diagnoses; section 11 ranks the five highest-priority changes.

## 10. Visual Creative Opportunities

For the major sections, specify what should appear on screen and why it adds meaning. Pay special attention to the first three seconds.

Consider talking head, B-roll, product demo, screen recording, before/after, text overlay, pattern interrupt, zoom/cut, actual comment screenshot, authorized review/testimonial or another proof element.

Visuals should reinforce or demonstrate the message rather than merely duplicate narration. Label assets that need to be obtained. Do not invent footage, customer comments, reviews or outcomes. Educational examples must remain clearly identified as such.

## 11. Suggestions to Improve

Give **exactly 5 concrete changes**, ranked from highest to lowest expected impact. For each:

**Current problem → Exact change → Expected performance impact.**

Name the relevant outcome: scroll-stop/early attention, first-three-second retention, watch time, CTR or conversion rate, as appropriate. Explain a likely direction and rationale; no invented numerical uplift. If the user asks for a rewritten full script, include it within this section after the five changes.

### 3 Hook Tests
