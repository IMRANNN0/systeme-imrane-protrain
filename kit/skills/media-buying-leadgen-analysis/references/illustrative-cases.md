# Synthetic illustrative cases

All names are omitted and all figures below are invented for method checks. They are neither course material, client results nor benchmarks. Currency is arbitrary units (CU).

## Submissions are not paid outcomes

A fully followed acquisition cohort has CU 2,400 media spend and 140 form submissions: 15 duplicate submissions and five invalid submissions leave 120 unique valid new leads. Every valid lead is assessed; 60 qualify. There are 90 reached leads, 30 unique booked leads, 24 attended appointments and 12 attributable paid new customers. In this simplified case each booked lead has one occurred appointment and every paid customer attended.

- Submission cost = 2,400 / 140 = CU 17.14; valid CPL = 2,400 / 120 = CU 20.
- Qualification rate = 60 / 120 = 50%; attendance = 24 / 30 = 80%; paid/attended = 12 / 24 = 50%.
- Cost per paid new customer using media only = 2,400 / 12 = CU 200. Call this media cost per new paid customer; a broader CAC needs its defined acquisition-cost scope.
- Compatible retained revenue is CU 7,200, variable fulfillment CU 3,600 and payment costs CU 360. Contribution before media is CU 3,240; after media it is CU 840. Fixed costs and other acquisition costs are unknown, so net profit is unknown.

A useful conclusion reports both the low-cost lead input and the paid result. A statement that CU 20 CPL proves profitability fails: it omits progression, revenue and costs. The counts alone do not prove which stage caused lost sales.

## Follow-up age changes the comparison

Two cohorts each cost CU 2,000 for 100 valid leads. Prior cohort A has 12 paid customers after 30 days; current cohort B has eight after seven days. It is invalid to conclude that B's final media cost per customer is CU 250 and therefore worse than A's CU 166.67.

Historical status events show A had six paid customers at day seven. The comparable recorded early yields are A 6/100 and B 8/100 at day seven; B still has unresolved opportunities. These observations do not prove a final winner, a causal improvement or a significance claim. Report early yield, open outcomes and sample limits; observe equal maturity or build a labeled forecast only if a relevant lag model is supported.

If A's day-seven history is unavailable, do not invent it from its current status. Deliver an age-mismatch finding and the needed CRM history or future observation date.

## An apparently good qualification rate has missing coverage

Of 100 valid leads, a sales team assesses only 40 and qualifies 30. Qualification among assessed leads is 30/40 = 75%; qualification status is unknown for 60 leads. Calling it a 75% qualification rate for the full acquisition cohort hides selection and missing coverage. The observed qualified share of all valid leads is 30/100 = 30%, not an estimate that the other 60 fail.

Next inspect assignment and assessment coverage before blaming source quality. A real sales-process change or contact experiment remains a recommendation until authorized.
