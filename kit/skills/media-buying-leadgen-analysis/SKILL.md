# Lead generation analysis

Connect acquisition spend to the business outcome the leads are intended to produce. Adapt to the actual sales process: services, local appointments, B2B sales and lead resale do not share one mandatory funnel.

## Scope and provenance

This is an original local proposed methodology prepared on 5 November 2026. The owner's sector-specific course material has not been supplied; do not attribute these rules to that course or claim its lead-generation track is complete. Adapt the method when reviewed material arrives, preserving useful definitions and documenting substantive changes.

Use the selected registered project's authorized sources and keep its data and artifacts in that lane. Reuse the existing brief. Ask only for unknowns that change the decision; give a supported partial result when possible. Shared skills and their examples contain no client records. Minimize personal data: use scoped pseudonymous lead IDs and aggregate outputs; names, phone numbers, messages and sensitive lead details normally add no analytical value.

An analysis request does not authorize new connections, data uploads/indexing, contacting leads, publishing, spending, CRM/account changes or campaign edits. Recommendations retain the task's existing authorization boundaries.

## Work from the business decision

Read [the protocol](references/protocol.md) for the data contract, pipeline/cohort rules and formulas. Use [the illustrative cases](references/illustrative-cases.md) when a worked example helps or when checking a proposed conclusion.

1. **Choose the outcome.** Define a valid lead, qualification criteria and the business event that matters: accepted lead, attended appointment, signed contract or retained paid customer. Record whether the business buys leads for its own service or sells accepted leads to a buyer. A buyer acceptance event is not evidence of the buyer's eventual sale.
2. **Reconcile the population.** Join spend, form/tracker and CRM evidence on defensible IDs, grain and attribution. Separate submissions, unique leads, existing customers, duplicates, invalid records and unassigned sources. Show coverage and control totals before reporting downstream efficiency.
3. **Compare equally mature cohorts.** Prefer acquisition cohorts with the same follow-up age and as-of date rules. Lead creation, status transition, appointment and payment dates answer different questions. An open opportunity is unresolved, not automatically lost; missing CRM outcomes are unknown.
4. **Locate the constrained transition.** Calculate relevant stage counts, eligible transition rates and time to progress. Inspect offer/traffic fit, qualification, contactability, response coverage, sales capacity and attendance as competing explanations. A lower CPL, a funnel drop or a correlation does not establish lead quality or causality.
5. **Connect to economics.** Use retained revenue or documented contribution on the same cohort and cost basis. Distinguish cash collected, contract value and recognized revenue. State missing fulfillment, sales, refund or fixed costs. Recommend a bounded reconciliation, observation period or test before a winner/scaling conclusion when evidence is incomplete.

## Deliver and verify

Provide the decision and scope; source/cohort/status/attribution definitions; coverage and maturity gaps; relevant formulas and counts; observations separated from hypotheses; business impact and uncertainty; and the next evidence/action with an owner if known. Keep the output proportional to the request.

Before delivery, reconcile spend and eligible lead totals, verify join cardinality and stage denominators, recompute aggregate rates from counts, check equal follow-up and distinguish observed outcomes from forecasts. State what would change the conclusion. Do not impose universal CPL, contact-time, follow-up-day or significance thresholds.
