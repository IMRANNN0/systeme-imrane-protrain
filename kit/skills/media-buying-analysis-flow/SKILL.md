# Marketing Analysis Flow

Locate a meaningful change, explain what the available evidence supports, and choose the next verification or bounded test. Start with the business decision and primary metric; the supplied e-commerce ROAS map is an example, not a mandatory framework for every business.

## Scope and routing

- Use the selected project's authorized sources and keep its data and outputs in that lane. Shared skills contain reusable methods and synthetic examples, never client data. Minimize personal data and preserve raw inputs.
- This skill does not authorize new connections, external uploads or indexing, publication, contact, spending, campaign edits or account changes. A proposed action remains a recommendation until authorized.

## Follow the relevant branch

Read [the diagnostic protocol](references/diagnostic-protocol.md) for formulas, denominator gates and candidate checks. Use [the output template and examples](references/output-and-examples.md) to make a reviewable result; scale the output to the question.

1. **Frame the decision.** Record the business model, outcome, comparison periods/cohorts, segment, primary metric and target provenance. ROAS is revenue/ad spend, not net profit. In lead generation consider qualified, booked, attended or paid outcomes and maturation; in affiliate work distinguish pending, approved and paid commission and acquisition cohorts. These are local adaptations, not supplied sector lessons.
2. **Verify the change.** Compare compatible sources, currency, timezone, event/status definitions, attribution, coverage and cohort age. Report sample and uncertainty. Recompute ratios from eligible totals; missing is not zero. A platform result need not equal retained business revenue.
3. **Decompose only where valid.** Use the smallest branch that can answer the question. The owner's e-commerce map is `ROAS → AOV / CPS`, `AOV → ASP × UPO`, `CPS → CPC / CVR`, `CPC → CPM / (1,000 × CTR)`, and `CVR → funnel / traffic context`. Those are conditional identities plus diagnostic hypotheses, not a causal chain. Never substitute sessions for clicks silently.
4. **Investigate alternatives.** Record observed changes separately from candidate explanations, counterevidence and missing evidence. Inspect only relevant funnel, offer, product mix, technical, creative, audience, auction and measurement factors. A drop, high frequency, low engagement or small sample does not identify a root cause.
5. **Recommend a supported next step.** State business impact, confidence, feasibility, owner if known, validation, and the decision that the evidence would enable. Do not declare a winner or implement optimization from the map alone. If evidence is inadequate, deliver an inconclusive diagnosis and a precise reconciliation or collection plan.

Before delivery, check formula units and compatibility, reconcile important totals, distinguish factual findings from hypotheses, and state what could change the conclusion. Avoid universal three-day, seven-day, frequency or significance thresholds.
