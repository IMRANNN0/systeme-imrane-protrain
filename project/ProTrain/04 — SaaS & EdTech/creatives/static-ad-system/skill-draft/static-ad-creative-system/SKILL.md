# Static ad creative system

Consult this workflow before production and again against the actual exported creative. Apply the project's current instructions and feedback within their stated scope. Preserve room for different art directions.

## Default messaging framework

For upcoming static image ads, default to **HOOK → PROMISE → CTA** unless the user specifies another approach:

- **HOOK:** Earn attention with a relevant problem, question, or desire tied to the intended audience's situation.
- **PROMISE:** Express a specific, realistic, verifiable benefit supported by the product and relevant to the landing page. Do not invent benefits, results, or guarantees.
- **CTA:** Name one clear next step that matches the actual funnel, destination, and offer terms.

These are three communication functions, not fixed rows, bands, cards, or a mandatory portrait layout. Let the visual argument determine the composition. Avoid generic educational posters that explain a topic without making the product's relevant benefit and next action clear.

Build hierarchy for mobile viewing: a recognizable first read, a usable promise, and a distinct action. Keep copy short and natural in the project's chosen language and tone. Moroccan Darija is appropriate when the project calls for it, not a default for every client. Preserve brand identity and verify mixed Arabic/French shaping, reading order, line breaks, and punctuation in the actual export.

Read [the review checklist](references/review-checklist.md) before production and again against the actual full-size and phone-size exports. Apply relevant checks within the user's authorized scope; a guideline update does not approve a creative or authorize production.

## Before production

Read the project's brand assets, approved product claims and offer, destination, and durable creative lessons. Find the latest owner decision on previous work; an earlier technical pass does not supersede an owner rejection. Record the sources consulted. If the project has no lesson record, establish a local structured record suitable for future retrieval.

Prepare a concise brief that identifies:

- The audience's specific situation, motivation, and proposed response. Distinguish researched facts from a persona hypothesis.
- The hook and the product mechanism it introduces. State the visual argument: what can the viewer understand from the picture that the headline alone does not explain?
- The available proof, its provenance, and what remains unavailable. Keep conceptual diagrams and educational examples visibly distinct from product output.
- The intended action, actual destination, and offer terms. An easy click and a completed purchase are different outcomes.
- The exact proposed text and a composition sketch. Assign each image, shape, label, and text tier a communication job; remove elements whose only justification is familiarity with an ad template.
- Format and placement constraints, unresolved blockers, and the intended experiment.

For missing screenshots or source material, prepare a conditional brief with an explicit evidence gap. Do not invent product UI, report contents, testimonials, results, or proof to fill it. Use the owner-approved claim source when website copy conflicts; surface the discrepancy for resolution.

## Choose the production method early

Use ImageGen for raster generation and editing. Read the available ImageGen instructions when invoking it. Preserve supplied original logo artwork unchanged.

When accurate typography, branding, or UI is required, choose a code-native editable composition from the start. Combine real assets and exact text layers with generated illustrative assets where useful. Obtain and inspect real screenshots before representing them as product proof. Do not patch a completed generated raster using Python image edits unless the user explicitly authorizes that method. An inaccurate generated logo is a production defect to solve with the original asset and an appropriate composition method.

Explore freely within the brief: portraits, paper, cream backgrounds, underlines, cards, and other motifs may work when they communicate something specific. Past failures do not create blanket style bans or fixed numbers of variants. Re-layout each placement deliberately when its dimensions change.

## Review the actual output

Inspect the exported file at full size and at likely viewing size. Re-read the project lessons during this review. Record the file, hash, date, dimensions, source assets, and unresolved defects.

Review two separate questions:

1. **Persuasion and visual meaning:** Can the intended viewer recognize their situation? Does the hook say something specific? Does the composition explain the mechanism or supply relevant evidence? Do people and objects have clear roles and plausible scale? Is the promised next action reflected in the CTA and destination? Does any decoration substitute for missing proof? Are the text tiers competing for attention?
2. **Technical fidelity:** Are claims and offers sourced? Are original branding and real product evidence intact? Are language, Arabic shaping when relevant, typography, contrast, safe areas, export dimensions, and reading order correct?

Report actual failed checks directly. A technically readable creative can still fail its creative direction. Keep owner approval, technical checks, and performance evidence as separate statuses. Do not invent universal AI-slop scores or claim a winning ad from visual judgment.

## Learn and test

For concept exploration, label the different angles and acknowledge that multiple variables may change. For a controlled comparison, identify one intended variable and hold other material factors stable. Select the outcome appropriate to the task; engagement or clicks alone cannot establish purchase performance. Avoid guaranteed CTR, sales, or winner claims.

After feedback, update the project's durable lesson record with the observed failure, prevention, review question, scope, and owner status. Retain contrary or still-valid technical evidence. Save preferences as preferences about the observed treatment unless the user explicitly generalizes them. Before the next ad, consult this updated record again.

Transfer only the reusable decision rule to shared guidance. Remove client names, paths, prices, proprietary assets, transcripts, and identifying campaign details. Do not alter unrelated projects or global visual defaults. This workflow authorizes preparation and review only within the user's task; publishing, spending, and messaging require their own authorization.
